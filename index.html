How evolution happens

Well-fed creatures reproduce. Their offspring inherit slightly mutated traits. Traits that help survival become more common over time.

Carnivores prefer herbivores, then smaller rivals. Hungry, aggressive hunters may risk attacking larger rivals, but the bigger the rival, the less likely the attack—and it can kill the attacker.

Orange rings show hunger. Hungrier carnivores are more likely to burst into a faster, energy-costly chase. Hungry herbivores may wander off course and briefly ignore their herd or predators.

Herbivores learn by surviving predator encounters: they spot danger farther away, stick closer to their herd, and make fewer hunger mistakes. Offspring inherit 75% of a parent’s learned skill as a simplified model of parental teaching. Average survival skill: 0%.

Size limits herbivore prey; fights between carnivores favor the larger creature. Creatures gradually slow as they age, reaching 25% of their inherited speed near the end of an 18-day lifespan. Death comes from hunger or old age.
